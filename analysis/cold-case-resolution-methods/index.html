How Cold Cases Get Solved: Resolution Methods

Of the 318 resolved cases in this index, 122 were broken in the initial investigation. Among the rest, genetic genealogy (25 cases, median wait 37 years) and DNA matches (27 cases) resolved the oldest cases, while confessions (24) and witnesses or tips (18) arrived at every age.

What breaks a cold case

For 122 of the 318 resolved cases (38%) the break came in the initial investigation, within a year of the crime, and the timeline records nothing more specific than the arrest. The remaining 196 cases are where the methods separate. Genetic genealogy accounts for 25 cases, all broken in 2016 or later, with a median wait of 37 years. A DNA database or direct DNA match accounts for 27 (median 21 years). Confessions account for 24: 17 of them came within a year of the crime and 6 came five years or more later.

Among the 50 cases that waited twenty years or more, the breaks came from genetic genealogy (20), other or not stated (14), dna match (14). 1 of them was resolved by a fingerprint and none by a witness or tip.

Method and caveats

  • Which cases. The 318 cases with status Conviction, Solved or Identified whose timeline holds a dated break event; 24 resolved cases without one are left out. See the resolution times report for the definition of the break.
  • Classification. The break event and the two timeline entries that follow it are matched against keyword rules, in this order: genetic genealogy (also matched on the case tags), DNA match, fingerprint, confession or surrender, witness, tip or informant, arrest in another case, and reinvestigation. A case broken within a year that matches none of them is counted as the initial investigation; one broken later that matches none is counted as not stated. Each case gets one method, the first that matches, even where several contributed.
  • What the categories mean. "DNA match" covers direct comparisons and database (CODIS) hits, including familial searches of offender databases; "genetic genealogy" covers family-tree work from public genealogy databases. "Arrest in another case" means the suspect was identified after being arrested or caught for a different crime, or after a victim escaped.
  • Selection. The index favours notable cases, and cases solved by new forensic techniques are notable. The share of genealogy resolutions here is far higher than in homicide clearances generally.

The figures are recomputed from the published dataset on every build, so they change as cases are added or their status changes. The rules live in scripts/analysis.py in the site's source. To report an error, email info@coldcaseindex.com.
