Gianni Versace

Fashion designer Gianni Versace was shot on the steps of his Miami Beach mansion by spree killer Andrew Cunanan. Cunanan had already killed four others across the country before targeting Versace. He killed himself on a houseboat eight days after the Versace murder. The case was dramatized in 'The Assassination of Gianni Versace.'

Case background and investigation

On the morning of July 15, 1997, the Italian fashion designer Giovanni Maria "Gianni" Versace, aged 50, was shot to death on the front steps of Casa Casuarina, his oceanfront mansion on Ocean Drive in Miami Beach, Florida. Versace had walked to a nearby cafe to buy magazines and was returning home at about 8:45 a.m. when a gunman approached and shot him twice in the head with a .40-caliber Taurus pistol. The attacker fled on foot and disappeared into a nearby parking garage. Versace was pronounced dead a short time later. The killing of one of the most celebrated figures in international fashion drew worldwide attention and shocked the design world.

Investigators quickly linked the shooting to Andrew Phillip Cunanan, a 27-year-old already sought in a multi-state manhunt. In the parking garage near the mansion, police found a red pickup truck connected to an earlier killing in New Jersey and tied to Cunanan. He had been placed on the FBI's Ten Most Wanted Fugitives list on June 12, 1997, weeks before Versace's death. Cunanan's motive was never established; police found no evidence that he and Versace had any prior relationship, though the two men reportedly may have crossed paths years earlier.

The Versace shooting was the final killing in a cross-country spree that had begun that spring. Cunanan's first known victim was Jeffrey Trail, a former U.S. Navy officer beaten to death in Minneapolis on April 27, 1997. Days later he killed David Madson, an architect, whose body was found near Rush Lake, Minnesota. He then traveled to Chicago and killed real-estate developer Lee Miglin in early May, and on May 9, 1997, he shot cemetery caretaker William Reese in Pennsville Township, New Jersey, stealing Reese's red pickup truck. Versace, killed on July 15, was his fifth and final victim.

A massive federal and local manhunt followed the Versace murder, one of the largest in the region's history. Cunanan evaded capture for eight days. On July 23, 1997, the caretaker of a houseboat about two miles north of Versace's mansion reported a gunshot; a police tactical team entered the vessel and found Cunanan dead from a single self-inflicted gunshot wound to the head, fired from the same weapon used in earlier killings. He left no confession or note explaining his actions.

Because Andrew Cunanan died by suicide before he could be arrested, he was never tried in court and never convicted of any of the five murders, including Versace's. Law enforcement formally identified him as the sole perpetrator based on physical evidence, ballistics, and witness accounts, and the cases were closed with his death. The murders were later dramatized in the 2018 anthology series "The Assassination of Gianni Versace: American Crime Story," renewing public interest, but the underlying question of Cunanan's motive remains unresolved.
