Jamal Khashoggi

Saudi journalist Jamal Khashoggi was killed and dismembered inside the Saudi consulate in Istanbul by a team of Saudi agents. Eight people were convicted in a closed Saudi trial. A declassified 2021 U.S. intelligence assessment concluded that Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman approved an operation to capture or kill him; the crown prince denies ordering the killing and has never been charged.

Case background and investigation

On October 2, 2018, Saudi journalist and Washington Post columnist Jamal Khashoggi entered the Saudi consulate in Istanbul, Turkey to obtain papers for his upcoming marriage. He never came out.

Turkish intelligence determined that a 15-member team of Saudi agents, including a forensic doctor with a bone saw, had been dispatched to Istanbul. They killed Khashoggi within minutes of his entering the consulate and dismembered his body.

U.S. media reported in November 2018 that the CIA had concluded Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman (MBS) ordered the killing, and a declassified U.S. intelligence assessment released in February 2021 stated that he approved an operation to capture or kill Khashoggi; he denies ordering the killing and has not been charged. Saudi Arabia initially denied knowledge, then claimed it was a rogue operation, then admitted the killing.

Eight Saudi nationals were convicted in a Saudi court (five sentenced to death, later commuted to 20 years). The case was widely seen as a politically motivated assassination meant to silence a prominent critic of the Saudi regime.
