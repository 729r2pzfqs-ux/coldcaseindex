Clare Fearon

Clare Fearon, a 76-year-old West Barnstable, Massachusetts, woman who has dementia, went for a walk from her Pine Street neighborhood on the morning of November 5, 2026, without her phone or money and did not return. A weeklong search by local, county and state agencies covered nearly 2,000 acres on foot and about 850 acres from the air without finding her. Barnstable police scaled back the large-scale search on November 12 and moved to a targeted investigation; they say nothing suggests the disappearance is suspicious and continue to ask for surveillance footage and tips.

Case background and investigation

Clare Fearon, 76, lives in West Barnstable, a village in the town of Barnstable on Cape Cod, and has dementia. On the morning of Thursday, November 5, 2026, she left on foot from the Pine Street area without her cellphone or money. Neighborhood security video recorded her at about 9:30 a.m., and police give about 10 a.m. as the time she was last seen. She was wearing black leggings or pants, a long-sleeve blue shirt and black shoes.

Her family reported her missing at about 3:40 p.m. that day, and a search began within the hour. Police later received reports of possible sightings on nearby Parker Road at about 10:30 a.m. and 11 a.m. on the day she vanished. Investigators established her movements before the walk and confirmed with the Cape Cod Regional Transit Authority that she had not boarded any of its buses.

The search drew on agencies from across the region. Barnstable police, Massachusetts State Police patrol, K-9 and Air Wing units, the West Barnstable Fire Department, the Barnstable County technical rescue team and the Cape Cod regional SWAT team took part, using drones, all-terrain vehicles, divers and more than 60 searchers on foot across roughly four square miles of woods, railroad tracks, cranberry bogs and ponds around Pine Street. Police asked residents and businesses to review camera footage from 10 a.m. to 5 p.m. on November 5 and asked volunteers not to enter the search area on their own. They also said reports circulating online that she had been found were inaccurate.

On November 12, 2026, after a week, Barnstable police said they were moving from large-scale search operations to a more targeted investigative approach. By then crews had covered nearly 2,000 acres on foot and almost 850 acres by air. The department said the change did not mean it was giving up and that resources could be redeployed quickly if new information warranted.

Police have said there is no information suggesting the disappearance is suspicious or that the public is in danger. Her family is cooperating with investigators and has asked for privacy. As of November 16, 2026, no public report indicated that she had been found. Anyone with information, or with security, doorbell or trail-camera footage from the area, is asked to call the Barnstable Police Department at 508-778-3874.
