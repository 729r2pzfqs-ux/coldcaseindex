Unsolved July 8, 2001 Homicide

Duran Bailey

Duran Bailey, a homeless man, was found beaten, stabbed and mutilated beside a Las Vegas bank building on July 8, 2001. Kirstin Blaise Lobato, then 18, was convicted twice, but a judge vacated her convictions and all charges were dismissed in December 2017. A federal jury awarded her $34 million in 2024. No one else has been charged with the killing.

Case background and investigation

Duran Bailey, a homeless man living in Las Vegas, was found dead at about 10 p.m. on July 8, 2001, in a parking area next to a bank building. According to the Innocence Project, he had been severely beaten: his skull was fractured, teeth had been knocked out, his carotid artery had been cut and his penis had been severed. No one has been held responsible for his death, and the case is best known for the wrongful conviction that followed.

On July 20, 2001, Las Vegas police received a call from a resident of Panaca, a small Nevada town roughly three hours' drive away, reporting that 18-year-old Kirstin Blaise Lobato had spoken of cutting a man who attacked her. Detectives drove to Panaca and recorded a statement in which Lobato described fighting off an attempted rape in Las Vegas in May 2001, weeks before Bailey died. According to the Innocence Project, police treated that statement as a confession to Bailey's killing. No physical or forensic evidence connected her to the scene, and relatives and neighbors placed her in Panaca on July 8.

Lobato was convicted of first-degree murder in May 2002 and sentenced to 40 to 100 years. The Nevada Supreme Court reversed that conviction in 2004. At a second trial, a jury on October 6, 2006, convicted her of voluntary manslaughter and sexual penetration of a dead body, and she was sentenced to 13 to 45 years. She maintained her innocence throughout.

Years of post-conviction litigation followed. At a hearing in October 2017, forensic entomologists testified that the absence of blowfly eggs on Bailey's body indicated he died shortly before he was found, in the evening, a time when Lobato was documented to be in Panaca; a forensic pathologist supported that timing. On December 19, 2017, Clark County District Judge Stefany Miley granted a new trial. On December 29, 2017, the convictions were vacated and the charges dismissed at the request of the Clark County District Attorney's Office. Lobato was released on January 3, 2018, after nearly 16 years in custody.

In 2024 a Nevada state court issued Lobato a certificate of innocence. On December 12, 2024, a federal jury in Las Vegas found in her civil suit that detectives had fabricated evidence, and awarded $34 million in compensatory damages against the Las Vegas Metropolitan Police Department plus $10,000 in punitive damages against each of two former detectives, according to KSNV News 3 and her attorneys.

With Lobato exonerated, the killing of Duran Bailey has no identified perpetrator. None of the sources reviewed report any other arrest or charge in his death.
