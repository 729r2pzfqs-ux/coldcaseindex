Daniel Robinson, a 24-year-old geologist, vanished on June 23, 2021, after leaving a remote water-well job site near Buckeye, Arizona, in his Jeep. The vehicle was found crashed in a desert ravine nearly a month later, but Robinson has never been located and the case remains open.

Case Background & Investigation

Daniel Robinson was a 24-year-old geologist who vanished from a remote job site near Buckeye, Arizona, on June 23, 2021, in a case that has drawn national attention. Born January 14, 1997, Robinson graduated from the College of Charleston in 2017 with a degree in geology and worked as a field hydrogeologist for an engineering firm, living in Tempe. Born without his lower right arm, he was nonetheless athletic and independent, and was remembered by family and friends as meticulous and devoted to his career. On the morning he disappeared, he was working at a water-well site in the desert of the far West Valley.

At about 9:45 a.m., Robinson left the job site near Sun Valley Parkway and Cactus Road, driving west into the desert in his 2017 blue-grey Jeep Renegade. A coworker he had met only that day was the last person to see him, and he was reported missing later the same day when he did not return. For nearly a month there was no sign of him or his vehicle despite extensive searches. Then, on July 19, 2021, a rancher found the Jeep rolled onto its side in a ravine on his property, roughly three miles from the work site. The airbags had deployed, and personal items — including Robinson's cell phone, wallet, keys and clothing — were recovered with the wreck.

The condition of the vehicle and its contents raised troubling questions rather than answering them. The family hired an accident-reconstruction specialist and private investigator, Jeff McGrath, who reported that the ignition had been turned over dozens of times after the airbags deployed, that the odometer had logged additional miles after the apparent crash, and that red paint transfer suggested the Jeep had been in a prior collision — findings he argued were consistent with the scene having been staged. Authorities searched more than 70 square miles of desert using cadaver dogs, drones, helicopters and off-road vehicles. Two sets of human remains found in the area, in late July and in November 2021, were both tested and determined not to be Robinson.

Robinson's father, David Robinson, a retired military veteran, has become the driving force behind the search, personally coordinating dozens of ground efforts and pressing Buckeye police to conduct additional forensic testing on the recovered clothing and airbags. He has said he believes evidence points to foul play, while investigators have not ruled out that Robinson crashed and wandered into the desert. The case became a focal point in the national conversation about disparities in media coverage of missing people of color, drawing comparisons to the widely publicized Gabby Petito case around the same time. Years later, Daniel Robinson has still not been found, and his disappearance remains an open and actively worked case that his family refuses to let fade.
