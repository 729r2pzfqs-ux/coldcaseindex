Unsolved August 24, 1951 Missing Person

Beverly Potts

Ten-year-old Beverly Potts disappeared on the evening of August 24, 1951, after attending a Showagon performance at Halloran Park, a short walk from her home on Cleveland's west side. One of the largest searches in the city's history found no trace of her. No one has been charged, and she has never been found.

Case background and investigation

Beverly Rose Potts was born on April 15, 1941, and lived with her parents and older sister on Linnet Avenue on the west side of Cleveland, Ohio. On the evening of Friday, August 24, 1951, she and a friend rode their bicycles to Halloran Park, less than a quarter of a mile from her home, to watch the Showagon, a traveling summer talent show.

According to published accounts, the friend had to be home before dark and left at about 8:45 p.m., while Beverly stayed to see the end of the performance. The show finished at around 9:30 p.m. The last reported sighting came from a 13-year-old boy who knew her and said he saw a girl he believed to be Beverly walking across the park at about that time. She did not arrive home, and her family reported her missing that night.

The search that followed was described at the time as the largest in the city's history. Dozens of officers were assigned to the case full time and large numbers of volunteers joined them. Searchers went through vacant lots, sewers and waterways, and the FBI circulated thousands of notices around the country. Police questioned known sex offenders in the area. Nothing belonging to Beverly was ever recovered.

Over the following decades a number of men came to police attention, but none was charged in connection with the disappearance. In November 1951 a man who had demanded money from the family was arrested; he admitted extortion, was cleared of any involvement in the disappearance and was imprisoned for blackmail. In 1955 a man arrested in Los Angeles claimed responsibility, but his account did not match the facts and he withdrew it. In 1980 two detectives disclosed that a tip received in 1974 had led them to a man who had lived near the park, but prosecutors concluded that there was not enough evidence to bring charges.

In 1988 Cleveland police tried to question William Henry Redmond, a former carnival worker then facing a charge in a 1951 child murder in Pennsylvania; he declined to discuss the Potts case, was never charged in it, and died in 1992 without having been convicted in the Pennsylvania case. Anonymous letters sent to a Cleveland newspaper beginning in 2000, in which the writer claimed responsibility, were investigated and came to be regarded as a probable hoax.

Beverly's mother died in 1956 and her father in 1970. Her sister, Anita, continued to look for answers until her own death in 2006. A local documentary about the case was broadcast in the Cleveland area in 2024. Beverly Potts has never been found and the case remains open.
