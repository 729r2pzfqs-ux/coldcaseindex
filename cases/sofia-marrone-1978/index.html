Sofia Marrone (Montauk Mary)

A woman found shot to death at East Overlook Park in Montauk, New York, on March 22, 1978, went unidentified for more than 48 years and became known as "Montauk Mary." On November 12, 2026, Suffolk County officials identified her as Sofia Margherita Marrone, 73, of Pleasantville, New Jersey, who had last been seen leaving a hospital in Somers Point, New Jersey, six days earlier. Investigators are examining a possible link to New Jersey serial killer Richard Biegenwald, who died in 2008, but no forensic connection has been established and the killing remains unsolved.

Case background and investigation

At about 12:30 p.m. on March 22, 1978, a U.S. airman on his lunch break found a woman's body at East Overlook Park, off Montauk Highway near the Camp Hero radar station in Montauk, at the eastern tip of Long Island. She was lying face up about 15 feet from the parking lot. The medical examiner found she had been shot four times, once in the chest and three times in the legs, and had probably been dead only a few hours.

Police described her as a white woman, thought to be in her 60s, about 5 feet tall and roughly 200 pounds, with short gray hair and hazel eyes. She wore a plaid tweed coat, a multicolored dress and orange slippers, and none of the clothing carried tags. With no identification and no matching missing-person report, she became known as "Montauk Mary." The case stayed open with the Suffolk County Police Department for decades, and investigators released an updated forensic sketch in 2025.

On November 12, 2026, Suffolk County District Attorney Raymond A. Tierney and Police Commissioner Kevin Catalina announced that the county's Cold Case Task Force had identified her as Sofia Margherita Marrone, born January 18, 1905, of Pleasantville, New Jersey. She was 73. Officials said she had left a hospital in Somers Point, New Jersey, on March 16, 1978, six days before she was found. She was one of six long-unidentified people named that day; officials credited advanced DNA sequencing of degraded samples followed by searches for relatives, with work by the county crime laboratory and medical examiner, private laboratories and the FBI.

The identification gave investigators a new line of inquiry. Tierney said Marrone was shot with rare .22-caliber short rounds, the same type of ammunition found with Richard Biegenwald when New Jersey police arrested him in 1983. Biegenwald, known as the "Thrill Killer," was convicted of murders in New Jersey, was known to pick up victims in Jersey Shore towns, and died in prison in 2008. A witness in 1978 reported seeing a bearded man in a gold-colored car with New Jersey plates near the park; Biegenwald wore a beard and had access to several vehicles.

Authorities have stressed that no forensic link between Biegenwald and Marrone's death has been established and that the homicide has not been solved. How she travelled from southern New Jersey to Montauk, and with whom, is not publicly known. Anyone with information is asked to call Suffolk County Crime Stoppers at 1-800-220-TIPS.
