Claudia Lawrence

Claudia Lawrence, a 35-year-old chef at the University of York, was last seen on March 18, 2009, walking towards her home in Heworth, York, and failed to arrive for work the next morning. North Yorkshire Police treat the case as a suspected murder. Several men were arrested in 2014 and 2015, but prosecutors found insufficient evidence and no one has been charged. She has never been found.

Case background and investigation

Claudia Lawrence was a 35-year-old chef who worked at Goodricke College at the University of York and lived alone in a terraced house on Heworth Road in the Heworth area of the city. According to a BBC News account of the case, her car was in a garage at the time and she had been walking to and from work.

On Wednesday, March 18, 2009, she finished her shift and was recorded on cameras as she walked home. The BBC reports that she was last seen at about 3:05 pm walking towards her home. That evening she spoke by phone to her father, Peter, and then to her mother, Joan, with whom she discussed plans for Mother's Day. She was due to start work at 6:00 am on March 19 but did not arrive. A friend, Suzy Cooper, waited for her that evening at the Nag's Head pub near her home, could not reach her by phone, and raised the alarm the next day. Her father reported her missing on March 20.

In April 2009 North Yorkshire Police said they were treating the disappearance as a suspected murder. The York Press timeline of the case records a reward from Crimestoppers, a televised reconstruction, searches at the university and in Heslington, and the jailing in 2011 of a man who made hoax calls that had prompted one search. The inquiry was scaled down in 2010.

The force's Major Crime Unit began a review in 2013 and re-examined her house. The BBC reported that fingerprints were recovered using techniques not available in 2009. In May 2014 a 59-year-old man was arrested on suspicion of murder and bailed; according to the York Press he was released from bail that November. A 46-year-old man arrested in July 2014 on suspicion of perverting the course of justice was also released. In March and April 2015 four men in their fifties were arrested on suspicion of murder. On March 8, 2016, the Yorkshire Post reported, the Crown Prosecution Service advised that there was insufficient evidence against the four and that no further action should be taken. The senior detective, Dai Malyn, said the inquiry had been hampered by witnesses who refused to co-operate or were not truthful.

The investigation has been in what police call a reactive phase since 2017. Peter Lawrence, who campaigned for the Guardianship (Missing Persons) Act 2017, known as Claudia's Law, died in February 2021. In August 2021 officers began searching the former gravel pits at Sand Hutton, about eight miles from York; the BBC reported at the time that nine people had been questioned over the years and none charged.

Detective Superintendent Jon Sygrove took over as senior investigating officer in February 2025. On the 17th anniversary in March 2026, the BBC reported that police still believe Claudia Lawrence was murdered, that her body has not been found and that no charges have ever been brought.
