Unsolved April 20, 1989 Missing Person

Patricia Bernadette Meehan

Patricia Meehan, 37, walked away on foot into the Montana prairie after causing a head-on car crash near Circle on April 20, 1989, and was never seen again. Thousands of alleged sightings were reported across the country in the years that followed, but none led to her. Her disappearance remains unsolved.

Case background and investigation

Patricia Bernadette Meehan, born November 1, 1951, was a 37-year-old woman who had been living in Montana, roughly 400 miles from the eastern part of the state where she disappeared. In the period before April 1989 she had reportedly been experiencing significant psychological distress; she had scheduled an appointment with a psychologist and had expressed a wish to return to her parents' home in Pennsylvania. A slight woman, about five feet three inches tall with blonde hair and a fair, freckled complexion, she gave little outward warning of what would become one of Montana's strangest and most enduring missing-persons mysteries.

On the evening of April 20, 1989, Patricia was driving northwest on Montana Highway 200 near the small town of Circle when her car crossed the center line and struck another vehicle head-on. Witnesses to the aftermath described an unsettling scene. Patricia climbed from the wreckage, walked toward the driver of the other car, and stared blankly, as one witness put it, as though looking right through her, without speaking. Rather than waiting for help, she then turned, climbed over a fence beside the road, and walked off into the open prairie, vanishing into the darkness. She appeared dazed and disoriented, and no one stopped her.

Searchers found a trail of tennis-shoe prints leading roughly three-quarters of a mile from the crash site, which they believed were hers, before the tracks faded into the terrain. Despite an extensive search of the sparsely populated countryside, no further trace of Patricia was found, and her presence in that remote area was itself unexplained given how far it was from her home. Investigators could not determine whether she had died in the wilderness, deliberately walked away from her life, or met with foul play after leaving the scene.

In the months and years after her disappearance, the case took an extraordinary turn: more than 5,000 reported sightings of Patricia Meehan poured in from across the United States. Based on photographs and videotapes, her parents verified sightings in states including Montana, Washington, South Dakota, and Minnesota, and several were confirmed as credible by police, yet not one ever led investigators to her. The case gained national attention when it was featured on the television program Unsolved Mysteries, prompting still more reported sightings. Decades later, none of the leads has been resolved, and Patricia has never been located. The sheer volume and geographic spread of the reported sightings, some hundreds of miles apart and years after she disappeared, only deepened the mystery, raising the possibility that she survived the crash and quietly built a new life somewhere far from Montana. Others have feared she perished in the vast, unforgiving terrain the night she walked away, her remains simply never found. Her case, handled by McCone County authorities, remains open and unsolved, an enduring puzzle of a disoriented woman who stepped out of a car wreck and simply walked into the darkness, only to seemingly reappear thousands of times across the country without ever truly being found.
