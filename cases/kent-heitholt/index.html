Kent Heitholt

Columbia Daily Tribune sports editor Kent Heitholt, 48, was beaten and strangled in the newspaper's parking lot shortly after 2 a.m. on November 1, 2001. Charles Erickson pleaded guilty and was paroled in January 2023; he has since said his confession was coerced. Ryan Ferguson's 2005 conviction was vacated in November 2013 and he was not retried.

Case background and investigation

Kent Heitholt, the 48-year-old sports editor of the Columbia Daily Tribune, was killed in the newspaper's parking lot in Columbia, Missouri, in the early hours of November 1, 2001. He had left the building after work and was attacked near his car shortly after 2 a.m. He had been beaten and strangled. Two members of the building's cleaning staff saw two young men near the car, according to accounts of the case.

The investigation stalled for more than two years. Police then received a tip about Charles Erickson, who had been 17 at the time of the killing and had spent that night out with a high school friend, Ryan Ferguson. Erickson had no clear memory of the night and had told others he worried that he might have been involved. Questioned by Columbia police, he confessed and implicated Ferguson. Both were arrested in March 2004.

Erickson pleaded guilty in 2004 under an agreement that required him to testify against Ferguson, and he received a 25-year sentence. Ferguson, who has always maintained his innocence, was tried in 2005. A jury convicted him of second-degree murder and robbery, and he was sentenced to 40 years. According to KMIZ, no physical evidence connected either man to the crime; the prosecution rested mainly on Erickson's account and on a janitor's identification.

In 2012 both Erickson and the janitor, Jerry Trump, recanted their trial testimony. On November 5, 2013, the Missouri Court of Appeals for the Western District vacated Ferguson's conviction. He was released on November 12, 2013, and the Missouri attorney general's office chose not to retry him. Ferguson later brought a civil rights lawsuit, which ended in 2017 with an $11 million award, according to published accounts.

Erickson's conviction remains in place. He has said that his confession was coerced by police, the prosecutor and his own attorney, as he told reporters on his release, and he sought release through the courts and the parole board. After a parole hearing in November 2021 his release date was advanced, and he left the Boonville Correctional Center on parole on January 9, 2023, having served about 18 years of his sentence.

The case therefore stands in an unusual position: one man remains convicted on a guilty plea he now disputes, the other has been cleared and compensated, and no one else has been charged. None of the sources reviewed report any new suspect or arrest as of 2026.
