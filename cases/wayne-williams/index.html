Wayne Williams Conviction Review

Wayne Williams was convicted in February 1982 of murdering two adults, Nathaniel Cater and Jimmy Ray Payne, and is serving two life sentences. Police attributed many of the roughly 30 Atlanta killings of 1979-1981 to him, but he was never charged in the children's deaths and maintains his innocence. Atlanta ordered evidence retested in 2019; no results have been made public.

Case background and investigation

Between July 1979 and May 1981, at least 28 to 30 Black children, teenagers and young adults were killed in the Atlanta area, according to the Wikipedia article on the murders. Atlanta News First reported that the bodies were left in Fulton, DeKalb and Rockdale counties. The first victims, Edward Smith, 14, and Alfred Evans, 13, disappeared within days of each other in July 1979, and the last, Nathaniel Cater, 27, disappeared in May 1981.

Wayne Williams came to investigators' attention on May 22, 1981, after officers staking out a bridge heard a splash in the river below, according to Wikipedia. He was arrested on June 21, 1981, and charged with the murders of Cater and of Jimmy Ray Payne, both adults. His trial began in Fulton County on January 6, 1982, and on February 27, 1982, a jury found him guilty of both murders. He received two consecutive life sentences. Atlanta News First reported that the convictions relied mainly on fiber evidence linking victims to his home and vehicle.

Williams has never been charged with or tried for any of the children's deaths. After his conviction, police attributed a large share of the other killings to him, at least 22 to 24 depending on the account, and closed those cases. Williams has consistently maintained that he is innocent. According to Wikipedia, a habeas corpus petition was denied and a federal judge rejected his request for a new trial in October 2006.

Doubts about the attribution have led to several reviews. DeKalb County police reopened a small number of cases in the mid-2000s under Chief Louis Graham, and that inquiry was later dropped. Wikipedia also reports that FBI mitochondrial DNA testing in 2007 on hairs found on a victim did not exclude Williams, and that later testing on hairs in the Patrick Baltazar case produced a sequence found in a small fraction of reference samples.

In March 2019, Mayor Keisha Lance Bottoms announced that evidence from the cases would be re-examined with current technology. ABC News reported in October 2021 that Atlanta police had sent decades-old DNA evidence to a private laboratory in Salt Lake City that specializes in degraded samples. In December 2022, families of victims publicly asked the city to release the findings, saying they had received no lab results; police said the investigation was ongoing.

According to Wikipedia, no results of the retesting had been made public as of late 2025. Williams was denied parole in November 2019, and his next consideration is scheduled for November 2027.
