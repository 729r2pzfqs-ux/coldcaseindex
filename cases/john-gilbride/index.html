Unsolved September 27, 2002 Homicide

John Gilbride

John Gilbride, 34, a US Airways baggage supervisor, was shot in his car outside his apartment complex in Maple Shade, New Jersey, and found at 12:08 a.m. on September 27, 2002. He had been in a custody dispute with his former wife, a member of the MOVE organization, and was about to begin unsupervised visits with their son. No one has been charged.

Case background and investigation

John Gilbride was a 34-year-old baggage supervisor for US Airways at Philadelphia's airport who lived in the Ryan's Run apartment complex in Maple Shade, Burlington County, New Jersey. He was found shot to death in his car in the complex's parking lot at 12:08 a.m. on September 27, 2002. According to The Philadelphia Inquirer, a gunman had fired multiple rounds through the driver's-side window of his Ford Crown Victoria, hitting him in the head and chest. The engine was still running.

Gilbride had once belonged to MOVE, the Philadelphia-based group, and had been married to Alberta Africa, a leading member. He left her in 1998 and filed for divorce, and the two then fought over access to their young son for roughly four years. In the weeks before his death, a Philadelphia Family Court judge granted him unsupervised visits. The Inquirer reported that the first such visit was set for the day after he was killed.

The dispute had grown tense. According to the Inquirer's reporting, in the two weeks before the shooting MOVE members fortified their house in West Philadelphia, held a demonstration in South Jersey, made accusations against Gilbride, and posted an online alert about the custody case. Gilbride's father, Jack Gilbride, has said his son believed his life was in danger, and he has long stated that he holds the organization responsible.

MOVE members have denied any involvement. The group's statements, as reported by the Inquirer, have included claims that Gilbride was killed by the government, or that he was not dead at all and was in hiding. A Philadelphia police official once suggested that gambling debts might have been behind the killing. None of these explanations has been substantiated by investigators.

The Burlington County Prosecutor's Office has never named a suspect or made an arrest. Then-Prosecutor Robert Bernardi told the Inquirer in the years after the killing that the timing of the homicide in relation to the custody proceedings remained a question for investigators, without asserting a connection. In 2009 and 2014 the office said the investigation was open but had no new developments.

In August 2021, amid renewed public attention to MOVE after former members spoke out about life inside the group, Burlington County Prosecutor Scott Coffina told the Inquirer that his office remained committed to the case and hoped that witnesses would come forward. No charges have been reported since, and the homicide remains unsolved.
