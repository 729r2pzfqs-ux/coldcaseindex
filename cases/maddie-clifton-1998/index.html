Maddie Clifton

Eight-year-old Maddie Clifton disappeared in Jacksonville, Florida, on November 3, 1998. A week later her body was found hidden in the waterbed frame of her 14-year-old neighbor, Joshua Phillips, by his mother. Phillips was tried as an adult, convicted of first-degree murder in 1999, and resentenced to life in 2017 with a review after 25 years. He withdrew his review application in May 2026.

Case background and investigation

Madelyn "Maddie" Clifton was eight years old when she disappeared from her neighborhood in Jacksonville, Florida, on November 3, 1998. According to WJXT News4Jax, police, relatives and members of the community searched for her for about a week. CBS News reported that one of the people who took part in the neighborhood search was Joshua Phillips, a 14-year-old who lived near the Clifton family.

On November 10, 1998, seven days after Maddie was last seen, Phillips's mother, Melissa Phillips, found the girl's body beneath the plywood that supported her son's waterbed, according to CBS News. She reported the discovery to police, and Joshua Phillips was arrested the same day. CBS News reported that an autopsy found Maddie had been struck on the head and stabbed at least nine times in the chest and twice in the neck.

Phillips was prosecuted as an adult. CBS News reported that the trial was moved from Jacksonville to Polk County. According to that report, Phillips said he had accidentally hit Maddie with a baseball, panicked, and then killed her to stop her screaming. The defense argued for a manslaughter conviction, but the jury found him guilty of first-degree murder in July 1999. In August 1999, at age 15, he was sentenced to life in prison without the possibility of parole.

In 2012 the U.S. Supreme Court ruled in Miller v. Alabama that mandatory life-without-parole sentences for juveniles are unconstitutional, which made Phillips eligible for a new sentencing hearing. According to News4Jax, Circuit Judge Waddell Wallace III heard the matter in 2017 and in November of that year again imposed a life sentence, describing the crime as one of the rare and unusual cases that justify life in prison for a juvenile offender. Under Florida law the new sentence carries a judicial review after 25 years. Wikipedia's summary of the appellate record states that a Florida appeals court upheld the sentence in December 2019 and that the Florida Supreme Court declined to take the case in June 2020.

News4Jax reported that in May 2025 Phillips, then 41, filed a formal application for the sentence review and was moved to the Duval County jail. Hearings were held during 2025, including one on October 21, and Maddie's family publicly opposed any reduction; her sister Jessie Clifton told the station that Maddie never received a second chance.

On May 6, 2026, Phillips filed a notice withdrawing his application, News4Jax reported. According to the filing, after reading the report of a state expert he concluded that he needed professional counseling about the crime and other issues before returning to court. The filing noted that the sentencing court keeps jurisdiction for the length of his sentence, so he may apply again. He remains in prison serving a life sentence.
