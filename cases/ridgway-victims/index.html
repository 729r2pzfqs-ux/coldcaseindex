Gary Ridgway Unidentified Victims

Gary Ridgway, the Green River Killer, was convicted of 49 murders in King County, Washington, through guilty pleas in 2003 and 2011. Several victims were known for decades only by numbers such as Bones 10, 17 and 20. All have now been named, the last being Tammie Liles, 16, in January 2024. Ridgway has claimed more killings, and in 2024 detectives took him to sites where he said other remains were left.

Case background and investigation

Gary Leon Ridgway was arrested in 2001 after DNA testing linked him to several of the killings attributed to the so-called Green River Killer, a series of murders of women and girls in the Seattle-Tacoma area that began in 1982. In November 2003 he pleaded guilty to 48 counts of aggravated first-degree murder under an agreement in which King County prosecutors did not seek the death penalty and Ridgway agreed to disclose what he knew, including where victims had been left. He was sentenced in December 2003 to consecutive life terms without parole. In 2011 he pleaded guilty to a 49th murder, that of Rebecca Marrero, after her remains were found near Auburn.

Some of the women and girls named in those pleas had no names at the time. Their remains were recorded by case labels, and Ridgway was convicted of killing them as unidentified victims. According to Wikipedia's summary, one of them, Sandra Denise Major, 20, was identified in 2012 after a relative saw a television film about the case and contacted authorities.

Genetic genealogy resolved the rest. In January 2021 the King County Sheriff's Office announced that remains found near SeaTac in March 1984, long known as Bones 10, belonged to Wendy Stephens, a 14-year-old who had run away from her home in Denver. NBC reported that volunteers with the DNA Doe Project traced her through distant cousins in genealogy databases. She is believed to be the youngest of the known victims.

In December 2023 the remains known as Bones 17 were identified as Lori Anne Razpotnik, who was 15, according to Wikipedia. The following month, on January 22, 2024, the Sheriff's Office announced that Bones 20 was Tammie Liles, a 16-year-old from Everett who disappeared in June 1983. According to the Everett Herald, Ridgway had led investigators to a site off Kent-Des Moines Road in 2003, where bones and teeth were recovered; the laboratory Othram built a DNA profile, and the match was confirmed with a sample from her mother. The Herald described these as the last unidentified remains connected to Ridgway.

The number of people Ridgway killed remains uncertain. Wikipedia reports that he confessed to about 71 murders, and NBC News has reported that investigators' list of possible victims grew to nearly 90 during the 1990s. Only the 49 cases covered by his pleas have resulted in convictions.

Efforts to find additional victims continue. KOMO News and FOX 13 Seattle reported that Ridgway was moved from the Washington State Penitentiary to the King County Jail from September 9 to 13, 2024, and that detectives took him to places where he said he had left remains that were never recovered. A court order unsealed in March 2025 disclosed the purpose of the trip; the reports did not say whether anything was found. In December 2025 the Washington Department of Corrections disputed a radio report that Ridgway was receiving end-of-life care.
