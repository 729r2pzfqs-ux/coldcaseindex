Sharon Tate & LaBianca Murders

Actress Sharon Tate and four others were killed at 10050 Cielo Drive in Benedict Canyon on the night of August 8-9, 1969, and Leno and Rosemary LaBianca were killed in Los Feliz the following night. Charles Manson, Susan Atkins, Patricia Krenwinkel and Leslie Van Houten were convicted in 1971, as was Charles Watson in a separate trial. Death sentences were later reduced to life.

Case background and investigation

On the night of August 8-9, 1969, five people were killed at 10050 Cielo Drive in the Benedict Canyon area of Los Angeles, the home of actress Sharon Tate and her husband, director Roman Polanski. According to Wikipedia's account of the case, the victims were Tate, 26, who was eight and a half months pregnant; hairstylist Jay Sebring, 35; Abigail Folger, 25; Wojciech Frykowski, 32; and Steven Parent, 18.

The following night, supermarket executive Leno LaBianca, 44, and his wife Rosemary, 43, were killed at their home at 3301 Waverly Drive in the Los Feliz district. The two crime scenes were not immediately connected by investigators.

Members of the group around Charles Manson were arrested at Barker Ranch in October 1969, and a grand jury returned indictments in the killings in December 1969, according to Wikipedia. Linda Kasabian, a member of the group, received immunity and testified for the prosecution. The trial of Manson, Susan Atkins, Patricia Krenwinkel and Leslie Van Houten began on July 24, 1970. On January 25, 1971, the jury returned guilty verdicts against all four defendants. Manson was convicted of seven counts of first-degree murder and of conspiracy, and Krenwinkel of seven counts of first-degree murder, according to Wikipedia and KESQ. Charles Watson, tried separately, was convicted on October 12, 1971, of seven counts of first-degree murder and one count of conspiracy.

All five were sentenced to death. Those sentences were reduced to life imprisonment with the possibility of parole after the California Supreme Court's 1972 decision in People v. Anderson invalidated the state's death penalty.

Atkins died in prison in 2009 and Manson died on November 19, 2017. Van Houten was released on parole on July 11, 2023. Krenwinkel, described by Oregon Public Broadcasting as the longest-serving woman in California's prisons, was recommended for parole by a state panel in May 2022 and again in late May 2025; Governor Gavin Newsom reversed the first recommendation in August 2022 and the second on October 14, 2025, according to KESQ, concluding that she still posed an unreasonable danger to society. She was 77 at the time of the 2025 decision.

Watson was denied parole for the eighteenth time on October 15, 2021, according to the case-archive site CieloDrive.com, with his next eligibility falling in 2026. He remains incarcerated at the Richard J. Donovan Correctional Facility in San Diego County, according to Wikipedia.
