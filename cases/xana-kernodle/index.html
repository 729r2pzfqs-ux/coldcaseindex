Xana Kernodle

University of Idaho junior Xana Kernodle was murdered with three classmates in their off-campus house. Her boyfriend Ethan Chapin was also killed. Bryan Kohberger, a criminology PhD student, was arrested in December 2022. Xana was found with defensive wounds suggesting she fought back. Bryan Kohberger pleaded guilty to all charges in July 2025 and was sentenced to life in prison without parole.

Case background and investigation

Xana Kernodle, a 20-year-old University of Idaho junior majoring in marketing, was one of four students fatally stabbed in an off-campus rental home on King Road in Moscow, Idaho, in the early morning hours of November 13, 2022. Kernodle, who grew up in Avondale, Arizona, and later lived in Post Falls, Idaho, was killed alongside her boyfriend, Ethan Chapin, 20, and roommates Madison Mogen, 21, and Kaylee Goncalves, 21. Investigators determined the attack occurred roughly between 4:00 and 4:25 a.m. Kernodle appears to have been awake, as she received a DoorDash delivery around 4:00 a.m. and used TikTok at 4:12 a.m. According to court records, she sustained more than fifty stab wounds, many of them defensive, indicating she resisted her attacker. A surviving roommate later told police she heard noises and saw a figure in dark clothing and a mask moving through the house.

The investigation, led by the Moscow Police Department with assistance from the Idaho State Police and the FBI, initially produced no named suspect and drew intense national attention. A key break came from forensic evidence: a tan leather Ka-Bar-style knife sheath was recovered near Mogen's bed, and investigators developed touch DNA from its button snap. Police said they used investigative genetic genealogy to generate a lead and then compared that profile with DNA recovered from trash at the Kohberger family residence in Albrightsville, Pennsylvania, reporting a strong match. Cellphone location data, surveillance footage of a white Hyundai Elantra seen near the house, and other records were also cited by investigators as tying the suspect to the scene.

Bryan Christopher Kohberger, a criminology PhD student at nearby Washington State University in Pullman, Washington, was arrested at his family's Pennsylvania home on December 30, 2022, on four counts of first-degree murder and one count of felony burglary. He waived extradition on January 3, 2023, and was returned to Idaho. A trial was scheduled amid extensive pretrial litigation, but on July 2, 2025, Kohberger pleaded guilty to all charges under a plea agreement that removed the death penalty as a possible sentence and included a waiver of his right to appeal. On July 23, 2025, District Judge Steven Hippler sentenced him to four consecutive terms of life in prison without the possibility of parole, plus ten years for burglary, along with fines. As of 2026 the case is closed with a conviction and sentence in place; related matters, including a criminal investigation into leaks that allegedly violated a court gag order, remained ongoing in public reporting.

Timeline

  1. November 13, 2022
    Xana Kernodle, Ethan Chapin, Madison Mogen, and Kaylee Goncalves are fatally stabbed at an off-campus home on King Road in Moscow, Idaho, between roughly 4:00 and 4:25 a.m.
  2. November 13, 2022
    Police respond to the home midday after a 911 call; the four victims are found dead and the investigation begins.
  3. December 30, 2022
    Bryan Kohberger is arrested at his family's home in Albrightsville, Pennsylvania, on four counts of first-degree murder and one count of burglary.
  4. January 3, 2023
    Kohberger waives extradition and is subsequently returned to Idaho to face charges.
  5. May 22, 2023
    A grand jury indicts Kohberger, and he is arraigned; a judge enters not-guilty pleas on his behalf.
  6. July 2, 2025
    Kohberger pleads guilty to all four murders and the burglary charge under a deal that removes the death penalty and waives appeal rights.
  7. July 23, 2025
    District Judge Steven Hippler sentences Kohberger to four consecutive life terms without parole, plus ten years for burglary and fines.
  8. July 2026
    Kohberger files a petition for post-conviction relief in Ada County District Court seeking to withdraw his guilty plea; on August 29, 2026, he asks that Judge Steven Hippler be disqualified. His conviction and life sentences remain in effect.
