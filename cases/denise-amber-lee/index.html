Denise Amber Lee

Twenty-three-year-old Denise Amber Lee was abducted by Michael King from her North Port, Florida home. She called 911 from her abductor's car but dispatchers failed to properly respond. King was convicted and executed in 2024. Denise's Law was passed requiring improved 911 dispatcher training.

Case Background & Investigation

Denise Amber Lee was a 21-year-old wife and mother of two young sons living in North Port, Charlotte County, Florida. On January 17, 2008, she was abducted from her home by Michael Lee King, a 36-year-old unemployed plumber who, investigators said, had been driving around North Port at random searching for a victim. King forced her into his Camaro, took her to his residence where she was sexually assaulted, and later shot her in the head. Her body was recovered from a shallow grave near a canal on January 19, 2008, two days after she disappeared.

The case drew national attention because of a series of failures in the emergency response. Five 911 calls connected to Lee's disappearance were placed between roughly 3:29 p.m. and 6:30 p.m. on January 17. Lee's husband, Nathan Lee, called after returning home to find her gone. At one point Lee herself managed to seize King's cellphone and dial 911, begging for her life so she could see her husband and children again. A key call came from motorist Jane Kowalski, who was stopped at an intersection and reported a child or woman screaming and pounding on the window of a nearby car. That call was routed to the Charlotte County 911 center and was mishandled: dispatchers failed to relay the information promptly or to properly dispatch the description to the officers who were already searching, and help did not reach Lee in time.

The publicized breakdown in the 911 system became a catalyst for reform. In the months after the killing, the Florida Legislature unanimously passed the Denise Amber Lee Act, which called for improved training and standards for 911 dispatchers. Nathan Lee established the Denise Amber Lee Foundation in 2008, and he has since traveled the country advocating for mandatory dispatcher training, uniform protocols, and public awareness. The foundation's work has been credited with influencing 911 training standards and legislation in multiple states, making the case a touchstone in national discussions of emergency-dispatch accountability.

Michael King was arrested the night of the abduction, hours after Lee was reported missing. He was charged with first-degree murder, kidnapping, and sexual battery. His trial was held in Sarasota County; on August 28, 2009, after deliberating roughly two hours, the jury found him guilty on all counts. The jury unanimously recommended a death sentence, and King was formally sentenced to death. He was convicted and sentenced to death for the murder of Denise Amber Lee. The Florida Supreme Court upheld his convictions and sentence in 2012 (King v. State).

After years of appeals, Florida Governor Ron DeSantis signed King's death warrant in February 2026. Michael Lee King, then 54, was executed by lethal injection at Florida State Prison near Starke on March 17, 2026. The case remains widely cited in 911 and public-safety training, and the Denise Amber Lee Foundation continues its advocacy for improved emergency-dispatch practices.
