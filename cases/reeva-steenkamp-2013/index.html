Reeva Steenkamp

Model and law graduate Reeva Steenkamp, 29, was shot dead through a locked toilet door at Oscar Pistorius's Pretoria home on February 14, 2013. Pistorius said he mistook her for an intruder. First convicted of culpable homicide in 2014, he was found guilty of murder on appeal in 2015 and later sentenced to 13 years and five months. He was paroled on January 5, 2024.

Case background and investigation

In the early hours of February 14, 2013, Reeva Steenkamp, a 29-year-old model and law graduate, was shot and killed at the home of her boyfriend, Paralympic sprinter Oscar Pistorius, in the Silver Woods estate in Pretoria, South Africa. According to trial records summarised by Wikipedia and to CBS News, Pistorius fired four shots from a 9mm pistol through a locked toilet door, and three of them struck Steenkamp.

Pistorius was charged with murder on February 15, 2013, and released on bail later that month. He has consistently said that he believed an intruder was in the toilet cubicle and did not intend to kill Steenkamp. His trial opened in the High Court in Pretoria on March 3, 2014, before Judge Thokozile Masipa.

In September 2014 Judge Masipa found Pistorius not guilty of murder but guilty of culpable homicide, South Africa's equivalent of manslaughter, and in October 2014 sentenced him to five years in prison. He was moved to house arrest in October 2015 after serving part of that term, according to Wikipedia.

Prosecutors appealed. In December 2015 the Supreme Court of Appeal replaced the verdict with a murder conviction, according to France 24 and ABC News (Australia). He was sentenced to six years in July 2016. After a further prosecution appeal, the Supreme Court of Appeal in November 2017 called that term shockingly lenient and increased it to 13 years and five months. Wikipedia reports that the Constitutional Court dismissed his bid to appeal in 2018.

A parole board granted Pistorius parole in November 2023, and South Africa's Department of Correctional Services confirmed he was released on January 5, 2024. According to CBS News and France 24, he remains under supervision until his sentence expires in December 2029, with conditions that include restrictions on movement, a ban on alcohol, anger management and programmes on violence against women, and a prohibition on speaking to the media. Steenkamp's mother, June Steenkamp, said through a representative that she was not convinced he had been rehabilitated, ABC News reported.
