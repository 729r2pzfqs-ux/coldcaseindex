Mary Jane Barker

Four-year-old Mary Jane Barker vanished while playing near her home in Bellmawr, New Jersey, on February 25, 1957, along with a neighbor's puppy. After a search involving more than 1,000 people, she was found dead on March 3 in a closet of a vacant house nearby. The coroner ruled her death accidental, caused by starvation and exposure.

Case background and investigation

Mary Jane Barker, the youngest of three children of Frank and Mary Barker, disappeared on the morning of Monday, February 25, 1957, while playing in a yard near her family's home in Bellmawr, a borough in Camden County, New Jersey. A cocker spaniel puppy belonging to a neighboring playmate went missing at the same time. An Associated Press report published in The Daily Iowan on February 28 said that the child was believed to have been kidnapped and that her parents had made a public appeal for her safe return, asking that she be left at any church.

According to the Wikipedia account of the case, which is based on newspaper coverage from the period, police were notified a few hours after she was last seen, and the search grew from a couple of hundred volunteers on the first night to more than 1,000 people. The press described it as the largest search South Jersey had seen. The same account states that the FBI joined the investigation, that a ransom demand of $500 was received by telephone, and that at least one man was questioned by police. No one was charged with any offense in connection with the case.

On Sunday, March 3, 1957, the neighbor's daughter and her mother went into a newly built, unoccupied house next door to the playmate's home. When a bedroom closet was opened, the puppy ran out alive, and Mary Jane's body was found inside, still dressed in the clothes she had been wearing when she vanished. According to the Wikipedia summary of contemporary reports, the house had been searched more than once during the week, but that closet had not been opened.

The discovery initially raised questions. The local police chief was reported as saying he suspected the child had been placed in the closet only recently, in part because of the condition of the dog. The autopsy, however, found no signs of violence or sexual assault and no evidence that she had eaten anything after the morning she disappeared. The Camden County coroner ruled the death accidental, attributing it to starvation and exposure, and investigators concluded that she had died about three days after becoming trapped. The closet door was not locked, but its inside latch was reported to be difficult for a small child to operate. The puppy was put down so that veterinarians could examine it as part of the inquiry.

In the days that followed, Bellmawr's mayor ordered that closet doors in new and rebuilt homes be fitted with hardware that opens easily from both sides, according to the same account. Mary Jane was buried at New Saint Mary's Cemetery in Bellmawr, according to her Find a Grave memorial. The publicity surrounding the search has also been linked to another Philadelphia-area case: accounts of the "Boy in the Box" investigation say that a man who had come across that child's body contacted police after hearing news coverage of the Barker case.

Because the official finding was accidental death, this case is not an unsolved crime. It is sometimes confused with unrelated missing-child cases, and no source supports the claim that she disappeared in Wisconsin.
