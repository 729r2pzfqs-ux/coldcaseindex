Which State Has the Most Serial Killers?

By raw count, California leads every published tally, whether it counts serial killers or their victims, followed by Texas, Florida, Illinois and New York, an order that tracks population more than anything else. Per head of population the picture changes, and Alaska ranks first. The published figures are quoted below with their sources and dates, followed by this dataset's own count.

What the published rankings say

  • As of September 4, 2016, the Radford University/FGCU Serial Killer Database contained information on 4,743 serial killers and 13,105 victims. (Serial Killer Statistics (Radford University/FGCU Serial Killer Database), Aamodt, M. G., updated September 4, 2016)
  • The Radford/FGCU database lists 3,204 U.S. serial killers, 67.58 percent of all serial killers in the database, while the United States holds 4.35 percent of world population (ratio 15.53); England is second with 166. (Serial Killer Statistics (Radford University/FGCU Serial Killer Database), Aamodt, M. G., updated September 4, 2016)
  • Radford/FGCU serial killer victims by state (2016 report): California 1,628 (1st), Texas 893, Florida 845, Illinois 629, New York 628, Ohio 433, Pennsylvania 420, Washington 390, Michigan 381, Georgia 365; total 11,465. (Serial Killer Statistics (Radford University/FGCU Serial Killer Database), Aamodt, M. G., updated September 4, 2016)
  • The 2016 Radford/FGCU state table compares victim rank with 2010 population rank: Washington ranks 8th in victims but 13th in population (+5), Louisiana 13th vs 25th (+12), Oklahoma 18th vs 28th (+10), the District of Columbia 21st vs 50th (+29), Alaska 39th in victims (51) vs 47th in population (+8), and New York 5th in victims vs 3rd in population. (Serial Killer Statistics (Radford University/FGCU Serial Killer Database), Aamodt, M. G., updated September 4, 2016)
  • WorldAtlas, citing the Radford/FGCU 2020 report, lists serial killer victims by state: California 1,777, Texas 984, Florida 933, Illinois 680, New York 677, Ohio 505, Pennsylvania 462, Michigan 425, Georgia 409, Washington 396; Alaska ranks 37th with 64. (The US States With the Most Serial Killings (WorldAtlas, Cory Price, Jan 3, 2024; citing the Radford/FGCU 2020 report))
  • 24/7 Wall St.'s 2015 analysis of the Radford Serial Killer Database (victims 1900 through September 2014, adjusted per 1 million residents per decade) ranked Alaska first in serial murder per capita at 15.65 per million (51 total killings), followed by Nevada 12.19 (98), Florida 9.92 (778), California 7.81 (1,507), Washington 7.44, Oregon 7.36, Louisiana 7.35, Texas 6.11, Utah 6.01 and Oklahoma 5.86. (The States With the Most Serial Murder (24/7 Wall St., Sam Stebbins, Oct 30, 2015))
  • The same 24/7 Wall St. analysis gave total serial killings through September 2014 as California 1,507, Texas 793 and Florida 778. (The States With the Most Serial Murder (24/7 Wall St., Sam Stebbins, Oct 30, 2015))

States in this dataset

Profiles so far are concentrated in California (16), Texas (11), New York (10), Florida (9), Ohio (8). The dataset is built in order of public interest, so this ranking will shift as profiles are added, and it counts a profile under every state where it records murders.

Method and caveats

  • Published rankings are quoted with their source and date. The Radford/FGCU database counts offenders by the state where they killed; some offenders killed in several states.
  • This dataset counts a profile under every state in which it records murders.
  • Raw counts track population; a per-capita view is needed before reading a ranking as a rate.

Figures from this site are recomputed from the published profile dataset on every build. The dataset grows in order of public interest and is not a census of serial murder; the national figures quoted above come from the sources named next to them. To report an error, email info@coldcaseindex.com.
