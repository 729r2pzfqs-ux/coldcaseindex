Roger Kibbe

I-5 Strangler

Killed in prison. Roger Kibbe, the I-5 Strangler, was convicted in 1991 of murdering Darcie Frackenpohl and pleaded guilty in 2009 to six more killings of women in Northern California. His cellmate strangled him at Mule Creek State Prison in 2021.

What the record shows

Roger Reece Kibbe, known as the I-5 Strangler, was a former suburban Sacramento furniture maker convicted of seven murders of women in Northern California between 1977 and 1987. A jury convicted him in 1991 of the 1987 killing of 17-year-old Darcie Frackenpohl, and in 2009 he pleaded guilty in San Joaquin County to six further murders under an agreement that removed the death penalty. He was serving multiple life terms when he was strangled in his cell at Mule Creek State Prison in Ione, California, on February 28, 2021, at the age of 81.

The earliest of the killings dates to September 1977. Lou Ellen Burleigh, 21, of Walnut Creek, had gone to meet Kibbe about what she believed was a secretarial job and was never seen again, according to Associated Press reporting. The remaining cases on which he was convicted all date to 1986 and 1987. Barbara Ann Scott, 29, was abducted in Pittsburg on July 3, 1986, and her body was left on a golf course in Contra Costa County, CBS San Francisco reported. Stephanie Brown, 19, was found in a drainage ditch in San Joaquin County in mid-July 1986, and Charmaine Sabrah, 26, of Sacramento, disappeared on August 17, 1986, after her car broke down along Interstate 5; the Wikipedia article says her remains were found near Ione that November. Lora Heedick, whose name and age are given differently across sources (AP reported Lora Heedrick, 20), was last seen in April 1986, and Katherine Kelly Quinones, 25, was found near Lake Berryessa in December 1986.

Frackenpohl, a runaway from Seattle, was found in September 1987 below Echo Summit, west of South Lake Tahoe in El Dorado County. Prosecutors described a consistent pattern: the women were kidnapped, bound with parachute cord, silenced with duct tape, sexually assaulted and strangled, and their clothing was cut with scissors that had belonged to Kibbe's mother. Investigators treated the oddly cut clothing as his trademark, according to the AP.

Kibbe was first identified after a woman he attacked in Sacramento in 1987 escaped. Police recovered a garrote made from two dowels and parachute cord, along with scissors and other items, and investigators later matched the cord to rope found with Frackenpohl's body and at Kibbe's house; all of it carried microscopic dots of red paint, the AP reported. He was arrested in 1988 for Frackenpohl's murder, convicted of first-degree murder in 1991, and sentenced in El Dorado County on May 10, 1991, to 25 years to life. For years prosecutors were unable to file charges in the other deaths.

In 2003 authorities confronted Kibbe with evidence concerning other women, and a San Joaquin County investigator later tied him to six more killings using newer evidence, including DNA. In 2009, at age 70, he pleaded guilty six times before San Joaquin County Judge George Abdallah Jr. to murders in Amador, Contra Costa, Napa, Sacramento, San Joaquin and Stanislaus counties, covering Burleigh, Brown, Heedick, Quinones, Sabrah and Scott. He received six more life sentences with enhancements for rape and kidnapping. Prosecutors said they dropped the death penalty because he was unlikely ever to face execution, and as part of the deal Kibbe agreed to help find Burleigh's body.

Searches with Kibbe were hampered by fires and landslides that had altered the terrain, a former San Joaquin County sheriff's investigator told the AP. In 2011 a Napa County deputy searching on his own time found a bone fragment in a dry creekbed near Lake Berryessa, the only remains of Burleigh ever recovered; she was buried in Klamath County, Oregon, where she was born. Kibbe never admitted to killings beyond those he was charged with, although investigators believed he may have killed others in the decade between his first and last known cases. The Wikipedia article also lists Karen Finch, found near Sloughhouse in June 1987, as a suspected victim for whose death he was never charged.

Shortly after midnight on February 28, 2021, an officer conducting a count at Mule Creek State Prison found Kibbe unresponsive on his cell floor with his cellmate, Jason Budrow, standing nearby. He was pronounced dead at 1:23 a.m. The Amador County Sheriff's Office announced on March 3 that an autopsy showed manual strangulation and classified the death as a homicide. Budrow, already serving life without parole for a 2010 killing in Riverside County, later received an additional consecutive life sentence for killing Kibbe, KTVU reported in 2023.
